Risks and open items
  • The brine transfer needs a written acceptance from MRC — it is the one interface that is not in RAMSIS scope.
  • MVR scaling on sulphate and silica needs the antiscalant regime proven in the pilot; the concentrator is one machine, so sparing is a spares decision.
  • Brine composition must be confirmed against the refinery brine system's materials before the detailed design freeze.

5 · Recommendation

Option 2RRO reuse with an MVR brine concentrator

Option 1 cannot be permitted: the blended feed carries 1,560 mg/L chloride and 813 mg/L sulphate against river limits of 600 and 400 mg/L, and no biological train removes them. Option 2 meets the limits but needs 108.2 ha of lined pond against a plot U9 of 1.4 ha (13,500 m²) — it cannot be built inside the battery limit, and the pond remains a permanent seepage liability. Option 3 meets every limit and removes the pond, but it carries the crystalliser, the salt-disposal route and the largest electrical load in the package.

Option 2R keeps the RO reuse of Option 2 and replaces the pond with an MVR concentrator that recovers 132 m³/h of the 165 m³/h reject as distillate, leaving about 33 m³/h of brine to the refinery brine system. Water recovery is 94 %, the disposal plot falls from 108.2 ha to 6,800 m² (0.68 ha), and the electrical addition is 1.79 MW against 5.97 MW for ZLD.

The recommendation is conditional on three things, all of them commercial or interface items rather than process risk:

  1. MRC accepts the brine transfer interface — 33 m³/h at about 25 % TDS into the refinery brine system, with the composition and materials agreed.
  2. The concentrator antiscalant regime is proven on this water in the pilot, because sulphate and silica set the achievable concentration factor.
  3. The reuse limits in RFQ §9 are confirmed against the raw-water RO inlet, so the permeate and distillate can actually be taken.

On the current estimate the recommended option is $48.58M of capital and $5.13M per year of operating cost, with a $98.90M 20-year life-cycle — the lowest of the two routes that meet the limits without a pond.

How this comparison is built
  • Flows and qualities come from the process sheets (P-01 to P-19): 70 % RO recovery, 385 m³/h permeate, 165 m³/h reject at 13,467 mg/L.
  • Pond area comes from the evaporation sheet: net evaporation 1.6 m/yr on 1,445,400 m³/yr of reject, with 20 % freeboard.
  • Thermal load comes from the ZLD sheet: 3,630 kW concentrator at 22 kWh/m³ and 1,815 kW crystalliser; Option 2R carries 35 % of the concentrator load.
  • Land is quoted in m² with the hectare equivalent in brackets; the thermal island allowance is 20 m² per m³/h of feed, the same basis used for the plot layout.
  • Capital and operating cost come from the estimate model at AACE Class 4, fourth-quarter 2026 Gulf basis, and are only shown in the internal view.
Open items that move the answer
  • Silica in the feed is not stated in the RFQ (CL-009). It sets the RO recovery ceiling, and therefore the reject flow every option above is sized on.
  • River limits versus the blended feed — chloride and sulphate cannot be met without desalination, so Option 1 stands only with an MRC deviation (CL-006).
  • Plot U9 allocation — the pond needs 108.2 ha against 1.4 ha (13,500 m²) available; a pond site outside the plot would be a separate land action.
  • Salt classification and disposal for Option 3 is still open with MRC; Option 2R avoids it entirely by keeping the brine liquid.
  • Brine system capacity — MRC to confirm that the refinery brine system can take 33 m³/h before the recommendation is frozen.
